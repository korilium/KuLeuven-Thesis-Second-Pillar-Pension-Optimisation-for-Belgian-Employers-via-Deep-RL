## Critical

### C1. The WAP fixing window is misaligned with the statute
- **Where:** `pension/economy.py:218` (`rows = start + mpy * np.arange(H)`) and `:249`; `pension/rates/wap.py:27` (`WAP_LAG = 8`).
- **What:** model year 0 starts at t0, the last data month, and its WAP window ends `WAP_LAG` = 8 months earlier. Eight months is the lag from the end of May to a **1 January** fixing. But t0 is not a 1 January: the rate legally in force at t0 is the calendar-year rate, whose window ended in May of the previous year. So every model-year fixing uses a window that is too recent.
- **Evidence:**
<!-- gen:wap_lag -->
- Before (`YEAR_START = "t0"`): t0 = Sep 2026; model year 0 ran Sep 2026 to Aug 2027, and its window ended 8 months before t0 (Jan 2026). The rate legally in force at t0 is the calendar-2026 rate, whose window ends in May 2025, 16 months before t0. On 2000 vasicek scenarios the legal alignment changes G on 37.5% of path-years (mean |dG| where different: 28.8 bp); year 1: 100% of paths, mean dG -25.0 bp.
- After (`YEAR_START = "january"`, the default): model years are calendar years; year 0 starts 4 months after the last observation, on 01 Jan 2027, and WAP_LAG = 8 is then the statutory lag (window of year k: the 24 months to May of year k - 1). G_0 = 2.75% on every path, equal to the formula on the observed window (2.75%); the FSMA published 2.50% for 2027 (the known one-notch difference, `pension/rates/wap.py`).
<!-- /gen -->
- **Proposed fix:**
  - **Option 1, simplest:** derive the lag from t0, as months from May of the previous calendar year to the model-year start, i.e. 12 + month(t0) − 5.
  - **Option 2:** compute the rate in force month by month on the calendar (fixings each 1 January), and hold, per model year, the rate in force at the payment date.
  - **Either way:** add a test that `G_0` equals the published FSMA rate in force at t0, and one that a model year straddling 1 January switches rate at the right point if Option 2 is chosen.
- **Status:** fixed. `YEAR_START = "january"` (default): model years are calendar years from the first 1 January after the last observation, so `WAP_LAG = 8` is the statutory lag. hull_white_p's α(t) is shifted by the calendar offset (`economy.year_offset`). `"t0"` keeps the old alignment. Tests: `tests/test_rates.py::test_wap_rates_of_scenarios` (both alignments) and `test_january_years_are_calendar_years`.

### M1. `LONG_RATE_P` means different rates in different models
- **Where:** `pension/params.py:87-90`; `pension/economy.py:145` (vasicek: θ of the 10Y), `:157` (vasicek_short: θ_P of the 1Y), `:183` (hull_white_p: long-run short rate).
- **Evidence:**
<!-- gen:long_rate -->
Before: LONG_RATE_P = 2.25% set the long-run 10Y under vasicek (long-run short rate 0.97%), but the long-run SHORT rate under hull_white_p and vasicek_short. After: the long-run short rate in every model; under vasicek the 10Y reverts to 2.25% + 128.4 bp = 3.53% (OLS theta 2.23%). Within the horizon: e^(-kappa 10) = 0.33, e^(-kappa 45) = 0.007 (vasicek kappa 0.1104).
<!-- /gen -->
  Under vasicek, θ is the asymptotic mean of the 10Y. With these κ the path converges largely within the horizon, but the early years are dominated by y_0.
- **Proposed fix:** define `LONG_RATE_P` once, as the long-run real-world **short** rate, in every model, and map it to the 10Y under vasicek (θ = `LONG_RATE_P` + spread). Alternatively, split it into `LONG_RATE_10Y_P` and `LONG_RATE_SHORT_P`. State "asymptotic mean" explicitly in the docstring.
- **Status:** fixed. `LONG_RATE_P` is the long-run real-world short rate in every model; under vasicek θ(10Y) = `LONG_RATE_P` + spread (`economy.vasicek_params`). Test: `tests/test_rates.py::test_long_rate_is_the_short_rate_under_vasicek`.

### M4. The caches ignore the data vintage
- **Where:**
  - `pension/economy.py:54-75`: `_CALIBRATION` is computed once per process, with no key;
  - `pension/economy.py:125-128`: the scenario key holds no data identity;
  - `pension/rates/data.py` `load_olo(refresh=True)`: refreshes the CSV but invalidates nothing.
- **What:**
  - After `load_olo(refresh=True)` in a running process, `rate_calibration()` and every cached scenario still reflect the old CSV.
  - Across processes this cannot happen: both caches live in memory only.
  - The scenario key otherwise contains every `Params` input it depends on: model, n, seed, horizon, `RATE_DT`, `BOOK_*`, `G`/`MU`/`SHORT_RATE` (constant), `LONG_RATE_P`, `SPREAD_10Y_SHORT`. The financing spread rightly does not enter: it acts in the numeraire, not in the scenarios.
- **Proposed fix:** key `_CALIBRATION` on the CSV path plus its modification time or hash, and include that identity in the scenario key. Alternatively, clear both caches inside `load_olo(refresh=True)`.
- **Status:** fixed. The calibration is keyed on `economy.data_vintage()`, and the scenario key includes it (`tests/test_mechanisms.py::test_scenario_cache_is_keyed_on_the_data_vintage`). The cache is also bounded (m7).

### M6. The credited return has an unresolved convexity term
- **Where:** `pension/dynamics.py:179`. In force, R ← (R + c)·e^{μ + σ z_R}, so its mean growth is e^{μ + σ²/2}; paid-up it is e^{μ}, with no shock. `dp.paidup_service` assumes μ. `checks.timing_neutrality` uses `SHORT_RATE` = μ as the neutral anchor.
- **Evidence:** the banded optimum's schedule at the two candidate anchors (constant rates):
<!-- gen:convexity -->
| convention, anchor | short rate | early (years 0-9) | late (35-44) |
|---|---|---|---|
| legacy (no correction), SHORT_RATE = MU | 3.000% | 7.92% | 6.78% |
| legacy (no correction), SHORT_RATE = MU + SIGMA_R^2/2 | 3.125% | 6.11% | 7.77% |
| DRIFT_CORRECTION (default), SHORT_RATE = MU | 3.000% | 7.28% | 7.54% |
<!-- /gen -->
  Neither anchor is neutral: the tilt changes sign between them. The test tolerance (25%, `tests/test_numeraire.py:41`, `tests/test_invariants.py:45`) passes both.
- **Proposed fix:** decide the convention.
  - Either drift-correct the shock (e^{μ − σ²/2 + σ z}), so that μ is the mean credited return in both states. That changes numbers, so put it behind a switch to keep the golden files.
  - Or document μ as the median log-return and use μ + σ²/2 where a mean is meant.
  - Then re-derive the timing-neutrality known answer and tighten its tolerance.
- **Status:** fixed. `DRIFT_CORRECTION = True` (default) credits e^{μ − σ²/2 + σz} in force, on the reserve (σ_R, or `SIGMA_R_RATES` in rate mode) and on the vertical liability (σ_L), and `dp.F_next` / `rho_next` follow. μ is now the mean credited return in force and paid-up. Re-derived known answer: at `SHORT_RATE` = μ the schedule is level (last row of the table above). The timing tests now allow 8% (measured 1.7%), and `tests/test_numeraire.py::test_legacy_drift_tilts_towards_early_funding` pins the legacy tilt.

### M7. Premiums are accrued at very different rates in the two rungs
- **Where:** `pension/params.py:102` (`SHORT_RATE = 0.03`) against `pension/economy.py:136-146` (vasicek r = y10 − 128 bp, θ = 2.23%).
- **What:** under vasicek the long-run short rate is θ − spread ≈ 0.95%. The mean A(0,T) is 1.905, an average accrual of about 1.4%/yr, against e^{0.03·45} = 3.86 in the constant rung. So a premium paid today costs about half as much in retirement-date money under the canonical stochastic model as under the constant rung, which moves the optimal level and timing. The spread is the full-sample mean (2000–2026), which includes the negative-rate years. (Numbers: `docs/DEBUGGING.md`, table "expected values"; `paper/explanations/model-walkthrough.md`, block `ce`.)
- **Proposed fix:** treat `LONG_RATE_P` and `SPREAD_10Y_SHORT` as joint calibration decisions and document the implied long-run short rate. Consider setting the constant rung's `SHORT_RATE` from the same anchor, so that the two rungs are comparable.
- **Status:** open. It needs the calibration decision on `LONG_RATE_P` (still `None`); no rate-mode headline run until then.

### M10. The certainty-equivalent moments mix levels and shocks
- **Where:** `pension/dp.py:260-275`.
- **What:**
  - `SIGMA_L` is set to the standard deviation of the **levels** of G_t, pooled across paths and years, including the time trend. It is then used as the volatility of an **annual iid shock** on a **vertical** ledger.
  - `SIGMA_R` adds the variance of the levels of μ_t in the same way.
  - The DP keeps a vertical ledger, while the true liability is horizontal.
  - This is documented as "a crude moment match", but the units mismatch is not.
- **Evidence:** the effect on the canonical configuration is small:
<!-- gen:ce -->
CE moments: G 2.458%, MU 2.631%; CE_MOMENTS = "levels" (legacy): SIGMA_L 0.693%, SIGMA_R 5.119%; CE_MOMENTS = "conditional" (default): SIGMA_L 0.000%, SIGMA_R 5.000%.

| policy (scored on 15000 vasicek paths) | joint | cost | stayer RR | avg contribution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CE_MOMENTS = "levels" (legacy) | -0.36749 | 1.4092 | 0.771 | 12.28% |
| CE_MOMENTS = "conditional" (default) | -0.36778 | 1.4177 | 0.775 | 12.34% |
<!-- /gen -->
- **Proposed fix:** estimate `SIGMA_L` from annual changes of the effective ledger growth rate, or set it to 0 and say so. Document that the CE DP uses a vertical ledger.
- **Status:** fixed. `CE_MOMENTS = "conditional"` (default) sets SIGMA_L = 0 and SIGMA_R = `SIGMA_R_RATES`: G_t is fixed on 1 January and μ_t is the book yield up to then, so a year's liability growth is known at its start, and the reserve's only one-year shock is the excess-return noise. `"levels"` keeps the old moments. `dp.certainty_equivalent` documents the vertical CE ledger. On the canonical paths the two CE policies score within 0.1% of each other, the conditional one marginally lower (table above). The change makes the moments consistent with a one-year shock; it does not make the policy better.

### M12. Tolerances too loose to catch real errors
- **Where:**
  - `tests/test_numeraire.py:47`, `tests/test_rates.py:97`: `|rel_err| < 4 se + 1e-3`;
  - `tests/test_numeraire.py:41`, `tests/test_invariants.py:45`: tilt < 25%;
  - `tests/test_objective.py:29`: 1% of |J|.
- **What:**
  - **Accrual.** The 10 bp absolute floor dominates in late years, where the standard error is about 0.2 bp. A closed form wrong by 5 bp there passes. The real signal there is the discretisation bias (+0.4 bp, see m12):
<!-- gen:accrual -->
| t | 0 | 10 | 30 | 40 | 44 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| rel. error (se), bp | +4.2 (22.4) | -11.2 (18.5) | +4.9 (8.4) | +2.2 (2.2) | -0.2 (0.2) |
<!-- /gen -->
  - **Timing.** The 25% tilt passes both anchors of M6.
  - **Objective diagonal.** 1% of |J| is wider than the effects it is meant to detect under "retirement" (the protocol-grid margins are below 0.1%).
- **Proposed fix:**
  - Accrual: drop the floor and test against the known discretisation bias, e.g. against a continuous-time reference or a weekly-step simulation.
  - Timing: replace the tilt test by a test of the sign change between two bracketing anchors.
  - Objective diagonal: test the diagonal on the protocol grid, marked slow, at 0.2%.
- **Status:** fixed. The accrual floor is 0.1 bp (no model needed the 10 bp), the timing tilt bound is 8% plus a legacy-tilt test, and the objective diagonal is tested on the protocol grid at 0.2% (`test_each_objective_prefers_its_own_policy_protocol_grid`, slow; largest measured excess 0.08%, `log`).
